Embodied Intelligence Full Pipeline & World Model Exploration

1. Human Imitation Learning: End-to-end pipeline covering data processing, action representation (LARYBench), pretraining, post-training, ICL (which is also test-time human imitation learning), simulation evaluation (on our self-built generalization benchmark), and real-robot evaluation (EVA-Client).

We have so far built out an initial infrastructure, including an evaluation protocol for world models and a version of an embodied world model that performs reasonably well in limited scenarios.
Core conclusions: human data can transfer to robots; latent-level representations are more suitable for robotics than pixel-level (video-generation) representations; the scaling gains from human data come from vision, requiring no action annotations; training vision and action with shared parameters in the foundation model yields complementary benefits (unlike the industry's MoT approach of splitting parameters so each modality learns separately); the ability to reflect and self-correct based on context history emerges from pretraining alone. Data is the core asset.

Multi-View World Model with Proprioceptive Feedback for Closed-Loop Policy Evaluation

Zijun Wang, Tianyi Yan, Haokun Lin, Panwen Hu, Bohang He, Siyu Ren, Xiaoyu Guo, Weijia Liufu, Shuoshuo Xue, FengJiao Chen†, Zipeng Ye, Xuezhi Cao, Zutao Jiang, Hanhui Li, Xiaodan Liang
We study whether an action-conditioned world model can serve as a closed-loop test environment whose policy success rates track those on real robots. Beyond realistic video, the model must return the camera views and proprioceptive state needed for the policy's next action and preserve failed-action outcomes to avoid overestimating policy capability. Across three policies and four local manipulation tasks, it attains a Pearson correlation of 0.95 and a Spearman correlation of 0.94 between generated and real success rates. These results support joint visual-proprioceptive feedback and failure-aware training as a basis for world model policy evaluation.

LARY: A Latent Action Representation Yielding Benchmark for Generalizable Vision-to-Action Alignment

Dujun Nie, FengJiao Chen†, Jun Kuang, Qi Lv, Xiaoyu Li, Xuezhi Cao
A unified framework evaluating the foundational problem of utilizing large-scale human video datasets to bridge visual signals with ontology-independent actions, comprehensively assessing latent representations on both low-level robotic execution and high-level semantic tasks across 1M+ videos and 151 action categories. Main Findings: 1. Action from human videos can transfer to robostic control; 2. Latent-level representation with diverse dataset is the key.

UniHetero: Could Generation Enhance Understanding for Vision-Language-Model at Large Data Scale?

FengJiao Chen†, Minhao Jing, Weitao Lu, Yan Feng, Xiaoyu Li, Xuezhi Cao
Could multimodal data can contribute more intelligence rather than text? In this work, we verify that Generation can improve understanding under large-scale pretraining (>200M samples). The keys are: (1) Only if you generate Semantics, Not Pixels. (2) Generation reveals a superior Data Scaling trend and higher Data Utilization. (3) Autoregression on Input Embedding is effective to capture visual details.

UniGUI-Bench: A Real-World Diagnostic Benchmark — What Capabilities Do GUI Agents Most Need?

Jiaxing Liu, Ruoqi Sun, Liya Ma, FengJiao Chen†, Ziwen Wang, Xuezhi Cao
A benchmark that jointly verifies task-level outcomes and process-level capability diagnosis. It spans 6 device platforms and 23 application scenarios with bilingual support, comprising over 10,000 evaluation items. Its core design links the two evaluation axes: each task failure can be traced to specific capability deficiencies, enabling fine-grained attribution beyond binary pass/fail.

UNO-Bench: A Unified Benchmark for Exploring the Compositional Law Between Uni-modal and Omni-modal in Omni Models

Chen Chen, Zeyang Hu, FengJiao Chen†, Peng Ding, Liya Ma, Jiaxing Liu, Ziwen Wang, Xuezhi Cao, Xunliang Cai
This benchmark is designed to effectively evaluate both UNi-modal and Omni-modal capabilities under a unified ability taxonomy, spanning 44 task types and 5 modality combinations. A general scoring model is incorporated, supporting 6 question types for automated evaluation with 95% accuracy. Experimental result shows the Compositional Law between omni-modal and uni-modal performance and the omni-modal capability manifests as a bottleneck effect on weak models, while exhibiting synergistic promotion on strong models.
